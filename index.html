The C.A.R.E.S. lens

Use this whenever the output is going in front of a class tomorrow — it's the fuller version of what the Prompt Builder already does for you.

ElementWhat to specify
C — Context & ObjectivesSubject, exact grade, curriculum standard (CDC Nepal, Cambridge IGCSE, NEB…), lesson duration, prior knowledge.
A — Audience & ToneStudent background, language level, desired tone (encouraging, inquiry-driven, rigorous).
R — Role & IdentityGive the AI a persona: "Act as an experienced Grade 9 physics teacher and curriculum designer."
E — Execution & StructureExact layout: slide titles, bullet counts, table formats, speaker notes, markdown headers.
S — Specific Constraints & SafeguardsExplicit limits: max 3 bullets per slide, cap the vocabulary level, include 2 formative checks.

Models drift over a long response — repeating a constraint in bold or capitals keeps them on track.

Refine & Fact-Check

When the first draft isn't right

Don't start over — a short follow-up almost always works better than a new prompt.

If the draft is…Say this
Too generic or dryRewrite using real-world analogies and a warmer, more engaging tone suitable for this grade.
Cluttered on slidesFormat into a maximum of 3 bullets per slide, 10 words each. Move explanations into speaker notes.
Too advancedSimplify to this grade's reading level. Replace jargon with student-friendly comparisons.
Too passiveInsert three turn-and-talk pauses and one movement or hands-on check into the pacing.
Missing local relevanceReplace the generic examples with local context — festivals, geography, trades, or everyday materials.
Too long for one slideSplit this into two slides, or cut it to the three most essential points.
